How to look up an NPI number, and what to do with it

By the Stethos team · November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The NPPES NPI Registry is free to search by name, NPI or location.
  • A registry match confirms identity, not where someone practices today.
  • Check the last-updated date and confirm with a second source before acting.

Every US healthcare provider who bills electronically has a National Provider Identifier, a 10-digit number issued through CMS. Looking one up is easy. Knowing whether what you found is still true takes a little more.

Three ways to look up an NPI

  • The NPI Registry website. CMS runs a search of NPPES. You can search by NPI, by name, or by location and taxonomy.
  • The NPI Registry API. The same data is available through an API, which is handy for one-off lookups from code.
  • The full NPPES download. CMS publishes the whole registry as a downloadable file, updated regularly, if you want every record in your own database.

What a result tells you

A registry record gives you the provider's name and credential, whether the NPI belongs to an individual or an organization, their taxonomy codes, a mailing address, a primary practice location and phone number, and the dates the record was created and last updated.

What to check before you act on it

Registry data is self-reported, so look at the last-updated date first. A record untouched for years may still show an old practice. If you're about to call, mail or route a patient, confirm the location against a second source: the practice's own website, a hospital directory, or the state licensing board.

Doing this at scale

Checking one NPI by hand is fine. Checking thousands every week isn't. That's the job an NPI lookup API like Stethos is built for: it starts from the NPI and returns licenses, affiliations and networks, each with a source and verified date.
