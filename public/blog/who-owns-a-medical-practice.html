Ownership

How to find out who owns a medical practice

By the Stethos team · November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Practice websites and firm portfolio pages are the fastest first check.
  • CMS ownership files flag private equity owners for hospitals, nursing homes, home health, hospice, rural health clinics and FQHCs.
  • Ownership changes with every add-on deal, so keep the source and date on each link.

Before you pitch a practice, it helps to know who really decides. A two-doctor office and a clinic owned by a private equity platform look the same from the street, but they buy very differently. Here's how to work out who owns a practice, using sources anyone can check.

Start with the practice itself

Practices that have joined a larger group usually say so somewhere. Check the website footer, the About page and the privacy policy, which often names the legal entity. Phrases like "a member of" or "in partnership with" are strong hints. Then search the group's name: platform companies list their practices and locations, and private equity firms list their platforms on their portfolio pages.

Check the public records

  • CMS ownership files. For hospitals, nursing homes, home health agencies, hospices, rural health clinics and FQHCs, CMS publishes the reported owners of each Medicare-enrolled provider, including anyone with a 5% or greater stake, and flags owner types such as private equity.
  • NPI records. An organization NPI and its authorized official can point to a parent entity, especially when several practices share them.
  • State business filings. Secretary of state records show the registered entity and its officers. A growing number of states also require notice of certain healthcare transactions.
  • Announcements. Acquisitions are usually announced by the buyer, the seller or the firm, and trade press covers the larger ones.

Watch for the signs of a roll-up

Shared phone numbers or billing addresses across practices with different names, a central management services company, a new practice management system or a wave of rebranded websites usually mean a practice has joined a platform.

Keep it current

Ownership changes with every add-on deal, so a list built in January is out of date by summer. Keep the source and the date next to every ownership link, and recheck when a practice changes its name, phone line or billing address. Stethos is built to do this for you: private equity ownership data links each practice, clinic and facility to its owner and platform, with the source and the date each link was seen.
