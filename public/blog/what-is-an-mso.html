What an MSO is, and why it changes how you sell to practices

By the Stethos team · November 7, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • An MSO runs the business side of practices, and in a PE roll-up it's usually what the investors own.
  • Vendor decisions often sit with the MSO and the firm, not with each practice.
  • Treat the platform as the account, and watch it for add-ons.

If you sell software, billing or staffing services to physician practices, more of your prospects aren't independent anymore. They belong to a management services organization, or MSO. Knowing how MSOs work changes who you call and how big the deal can be.

What an MSO is

An MSO runs the business side of medical practices: billing and revenue cycle, IT, HR, purchasing, payer contracting and compliance. Many states limit who can own a medical practice, so a common structure is that physicians own the practice, investors own the MSO, and a long-term management agreement ties the two together.

Private equity roll-ups usually follow this pattern. A firm buys a large "platform" group, builds an MSO around it, then adds smaller practices as add-ons, each plugged into the same back office.

Why it matters if you sell to practices

  • The decision moves up. Billing, software and vendors are often chosen centrally, by MSO executives and the firm's operating partners rather than each office manager.
  • One deal, many sites. Winning an MSO can open every practice it manages, from a handful of sites to hundreds.
  • A different pace. PE-backed groups often decide faster than hospital joint ventures, and many prefer one vendor across all sites to a set of point solutions.
  • Timing follows deals. A new platform or a wave of add-ons is when back-office decisions get made.

How to work an MSO as an account

Treat the platform as the account and each practice as a site under it. Map every practice the MSO manages by state and metro, and find the people who decide for all of them: the MSO's CEO, CFO and heads of revenue cycle and operations, plus the firm's healthcare operating partners. Then watch for add-ons, because each new practice is a new site on a deal you already have.

Stethos is built for this. Private equity ownership data maps each firm's platforms and every site they own, and your agent can watch a platform for new add-ons. To check a single practice first, see how to find out who owns a medical practice.
