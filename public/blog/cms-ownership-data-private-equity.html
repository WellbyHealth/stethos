What CMS ownership files show about private equity in healthcare

By the Stethos team · November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • CMS publishes owners for hospitals, nursing homes, home health, hospice, rural health clinics and FQHCs.
  • Owner-type flags include private equity, REITs, holding companies and management companies.
  • Physician practices and surgery centers aren't covered, so other sources fill the gap.

Most provider data tells you what a provider does. CMS ownership files tell you who owns it. For several kinds of Medicare-enrolled facilities, CMS publishes every reported owner and flags which ones are private equity companies. Here's what's in the files, and where they stop.

What CMS publishes

CMS releases "All Owners" datasets for hospitals, skilled nursing facilities, home health agencies, hospices, rural health clinics and federally qualified health centers. Each row links a Medicare enrollment to one owner, an individual or an organization, with the owner's role, the date the association started and, where reported, the percentage owned.

Roles cover direct and indirect ownership of 5% or more, mortgage and security interests, and operational or managerial control. That last one matters: a company that manages a facility can appear even if it owns none of it.

The flags that matter

For organizational owners, the files carry yes-or-no flags for the type of organization, including:

  • Private equity company
  • Investment firm and holding company
  • Real estate investment trust (REIT)
  • Management services company and chain home office
  • Created for acquisition, for entities set up to buy the provider

Together, the flags let you trace a facility up through its holding companies to the firm behind it, then find every other facility the same owner touches.

Where the files fall short

  • No physician practices or surgery centers. The datasets don't cover physician groups, ambulatory surgery centers or most outpatient clinics, which is where many roll-ups happen.
  • Self-reported. Owners report through Medicare enrollment. Flags can be blank, and changes appear when enrollment is updated.
  • Names, not structures. The same firm can appear under different fund and holding-company names, so linking them takes work.

Filling the gaps

For practices and surgery centers, ownership comes from firm portfolio pages, acquisition announcements, practice websites and state filings. Stethos is built to combine those with the CMS files into one map of who owns which clinics, and where, with the source and date on every link.
