See which private equity firms own which clinics, and where.

Private equity has rolled up practices, surgery centers, home health agencies and clinics across the US, usually as a platform company plus a string of add-ons under one management services organization (MSO). Stethos is built to map that structure: the firm, its platforms, every site they own and the markets they're in, so your agent can work a roll-up as one account and find the people who decide for all of its sites.

FAQ

Private equity ownership data: common questions

Related: Docs: data sources · What CMS ownership files show · Healthcare investors

Where does ownership data come from?

For hospitals, nursing homes, home health agencies, hospices, rural health clinics and FQHCs, CMS publishes ownership files that list each owner and flag owner types such as private equity. For physician practices, surgery centers and MSOs, which those files don't cover, Stethos is built to combine firm portfolio pages, acquisition announcements and state filings. Every link carries its source and the date it was seen.

Who is this for?

Teams that sell to multi-site, PE-backed groups: revenue cycle, billing, staffing, software and services vendors. Selling to one MSO can open dozens of sites, and the decision usually sits with the MSO and the firm's operating partners rather than each clinic.

Can I see the people, not just the companies?

Yes. Stethos is built to return MSO executives and the firm's healthcare deal and operating partners, with business contact details where they're published.

How current is it?

CMS updates its ownership files regularly, and acquisitions are picked up when they're announced. Your agent can watch a firm or a platform and hear about new add-ons as they happen.

Does it include patient data?

No. Stethos holds data about organizations, their owners and the people who run them only.
